# Legal, safety, and equity guardrails

## Not legal advice

ShelterWise is an intake and navigation aid. It must not claim to be a lawyer, establish an attorney-client relationship, guarantee a legal outcome, or make a definitive determination of rights or program eligibility.

## Legal content

- Prefer current official statutes, court pages, housing authority pages, and government program sources.
- Display jurisdiction and effective/last-checked date.
- Link to the original source.
- Quote only the minimum needed; summarize in plain language.
- Flag deadlines and procedural requirements for immediate qualified review.
- If law is ambiguous or sources conflict, say so and escalate to a human.
- Do not invent citations, statutes, forms, phone numbers, or program availability.

## Immediate safety

If a user reports immediate danger (for example, active fire, exposed live wiring, gas leak, or immediate threat), prioritize contacting local emergency services or the relevant urgent authority. Do not let the agent delay emergency action while gathering more details. The exact guidance must be localized and reviewed.

## Evidence boundaries

A public complaint near an address is not proof of a tenant's unit condition. A lack of public complaints is not proof that no issue exists. Keep tenant statements and public evidence visibly separate.

## Fairness and protected traits

- Never use race, ethnicity, religion, disability, immigration status, sex, or other protected/sensitive traits to rank an individual tenant, decide eligibility, or prioritize case handling.
- Aggregate neighborhood indicators may be used to identify service gaps or under-reporting patterns, with careful caveats.
- Do not infer an individual's protected traits from geography.
- Explain the limitations of aggregate data and avoid stigmatizing neighborhoods.

## Human review

Before the brief is used for a legal, financial, or housing decision, a qualified person must verify facts, current law, deadlines, and program requirements.
